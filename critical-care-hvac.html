Air system components

How the clinical environment is controlled

Critical-care HVAC is more than cooling. The complete air path—from plant and filtration to room supply, return and exhaust—must match the approved room function.

01

Air-handling & filtration

AHUs, fan sections and specified filter stages condition and clean the supply air before it reaches clinical rooms.

02

Ductwork & air distribution

Duct routes, dampers, insulation, diffusers and return/exhaust paths distribute air to the required rooms and zones.

03

Room controls & pressure

Sensors, control valves and balancing devices support the temperature, humidity and pressure relationships specified for each clinical space.

Inspection & commissioning

HVAC tests that demonstrate room performance

Use calibrated instruments and record the room, operating mode, measurement point and acceptance value for each check required by the project.

01 · DUCTWORK

Duct leakage / pressure test

Test duct sections to the specified leakage class or project procedure before insulation and closure.

02 · AIRFLOW

Air balancing & air-change rate

Measure and balance supply, return and exhaust air at terminals; compare the resulting air-change rate with the approved room criteria.

03 · PRESSURE

Differential pressure & airflow direction

Verify the intended pressure relationship between adjacent rooms and confirm the designed direction of airflow.

04 · ENVIRONMENT

Temperature & relative humidity

Record room conditions under the agreed operating mode and confirm that control settings maintain the specified range.

05 · FILTRATION

Filter integrity / HEPA scan

Where terminal HEPA filters are specified, perform the applicable integrity or leak test and retain the filter identification and results.

06 · CONTROLS

Controls, sensors & alarms

Function-check sensors, actuators, interlocks and BMS points included in the design; confirm alarm display and response.

These are typical verification points, not a claim that every test has been completed on every project. The approved design, applicable code, equipment manufacturer and contract scope determine the required tests, acceptance limits and authorised sign-off.

Project records

HVAC evidence for operations and maintenance

The handover should show how the system was balanced, what equipment was installed and how the facility team can maintain it.

System information

  • AHU, fan, filter and control-point schedules
  • Duct and equipment as-built drawings
  • Filter grades, change intervals and O&M information

Test & commissioning records

  • Duct leakage and equipment start-up sheets
  • Air-balancing and room-condition reports
  • Pressure, HEPA integrity and BMS functional records where specified
